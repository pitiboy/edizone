# Initial design prompt (preserved)

Create a detailed engineering design and technical sketch of a DIY automatic chicken feeder for 10–20 chickens.

The system should use a 20–30 liter food-safe hopper with a steep 60-degree conical bottom to prevent feed from bridging or getting stuck.

At the bottom, design a rotary metering wheel with 6 individual dosing compartments. The wheel should be approximately 120 mm in diameter, with each compartment approximately 25 mm deep. A 65 mm feed outlet should allow the compartments to fill reliably.

Drive the wheel using a 12 V low-speed geared DC motor. Add a Hall-effect sensor and magnet to detect each complete rotation so the controller knows exactly how many portions have been dispensed.

Above the dosing wheel, include an anti-jamming mechanism, such as a flexible wiper/agitator that gently moves the feed whenever the wheel rotates. The design should prevent seeds or pellets from becoming wedged in the outlet or between the rotating wheel and housing.

Include a 12 V controller/timer that can activate the feeder at programmable times, for example twice per day. The system should also have a manual dispensing button.

The feed should travel:

hopper → gravity-fed conical section → metering wheel → outlet chute → covered chicken feeding trough

Include:

* a full isometric cutaway view
* a side cross-section
* a top view
* a detailed view of the anti-jam mechanism
* a detailed view of the rotary dosing wheel
* arrows showing the direction of feed flow and wheel rotation
* dimensions for all important components
* mounting points and screw locations
* motor and sensor positions
* wiring diagram for the motor, Hall sensor, controller, and 12 V power supply
* an exploded assembly view

The design should prioritize reliability, easy cleaning, moisture resistance, low power consumption, and prevention of feed blockage. Use realistic engineering proportions and components that could actually be purchased or 3D-printed/machined by a hobbyist.

Label every component clearly and provide a numbered bill of materials with suggested materials, dimensions, and specifications.

Do not make it merely conceptual or decorative. Treat it as a practical prototype that could actually be built.
